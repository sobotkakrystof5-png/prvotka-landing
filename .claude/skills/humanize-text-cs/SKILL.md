# Humanize Text (Czech)

This skill rewrites or reviews text so it doesn't read as generated by a language model. The goal isn't to deceive anyone about authorship — it's to strip out the mechanical stylistic tics that show up systematically in AI-generated text and that people easily recognize.

Target language for this skill's rewrites: **Czech**. The vocabulary table in step 2 and in the "Detailed patterns" section below is Czech-specific. If the user's text is in a different language, apply the same underlying principles (dash-as-connector, stock phrases, tri-colons, uniform rhythm, hedging tone) but find that language's own equivalent stock phrases rather than using the Czech list.

## When to use this skill

- User wants text to sound "more human," get rid of "AI style," "AI dashes," etc.
- Text is meant for publication, school submission, a job application, or anywhere it matters that it doesn't read as machine-written.
- A general style/proofreading request that mentions naturalness or worry about AI detection.
- Before handing back any generated text the user will use externally (email, essay, post, cover letter), if they've shown interest in this earlier in the conversation.

## Procedure

### Step 1 — Get the text

If the text isn't already in the conversation, ask the user to paste it or upload a file. Never invent content on their behalf.

### Step 2 — Scan the text against the checklist categories

Go through the text and flag/fix instances in these five categories. Detailed examples and explanations for each category live in the "Detailed patterns" section at the end of this document — check it before editing any text that's long or stylistically sensitive.

1. **Punctuation** — dashes (em/en dash) used as a connector inside a sentence; overused colons and semicolons.
2. **Structure/formatting** — unnecessary bullet lists, mid-sentence bold text, mechanical closing summaries, artificial headers like "Závěr" (Conclusion).
3. **Vocabulary and phrasing** — stock words/phrases (see table in "Detailed patterns" below), the "nejde jen o X, ale i o Y" construction, overused emphasis adjectives ("klíčový," "zásadní").
4. **Syntax and rhythm** — consecutive sentences of similar length, tri-colon lists (three items in a row), lack of natural irregularity.
5. **Tone** — an overly balanced/diplomatic tone, absence of personal opinion or emotion, excessive politeness, closing with an unsolicited offer of help ("dejte mi vědět, kdybyste...").

### Step 3 — Rewrite the text

Adjust the text so that it:
- Keeps the **same meaning and facts** — don't add or remove content.
- Varies sentence length and uses more natural connectors instead of dashes.
- Drops formatting crutches (bold text, unnecessary bullets) where they serve no functional purpose.
- Replaces or cuts stock phrases.
- Optionally includes a small personal aside, mild informality, or a clearer stance where it fits the tone and purpose of the text (be more conservative with formal/academic text — humanizing doesn't always mean making it casual).

### Step 4 — Show the result

If the text is under ~200 words, show it directly in the conversation (not as a separate file/artifact). For longer text, or if the user wants it as a standalone document, save the result as a file.

Briefly (1–2 sentences) summarize what changed overall — don't list every single edit unless the user explicitly asks for that.

### Step 5 (optional) — Final check

If the user wants it, run through the "Final checklist" at the end of this document once more and confirm none of the main patterns remain.

## Important principles

- **Never change facts, numbers, quotes, or the meaning of the text** — this is purely stylistic.
- **Preserve the author's voice.** If the text has a distinct personal style (technical jargon, specific humor), keep it — humanizing shouldn't flatten it into a neutral tone.
- For formal/academic text (theses, legal writing), be conservative about introducing informality — the priority is removing mechanical tics (dashes, tri-colons, stock phrases), not shifting register.
- This skill isn't meant to help evade plagiarism/AI-detection in a way that violates an institution's academic integrity rules. If context makes clear the user intends to submit someone else's or AI-generated work as their own in violation of their institution's rules, note that reservation in your response, or ask for clarification, while still only doing a stylistic pass.

# Detailed patterns

The section below contains the full list of patterns with "before/after" examples for each category, plus a final checklist. Same content as summarized above, just with concrete examples.

---

## 1. Punctuation

**Pattern:** Dash (em dash „—", en dash „–") mid-sentence instead of a comma, period, or conjunction.

Example:
- Before: „Práce z domova má své výhody – flexibilitu, úsporu času i peněz – ale zároveň přináší nová úskalí."
- After: „Práce z domova má své výhody. Přináší flexibilitu a šetří čas i peníze. Zároveň s sebou ale nese nová úskalí."

**Pattern:** Overused colons to introduce a list, even where a plain sentence would do.

Example:
- Before: „Tento přístup má tři výhody: je rychlý, levný a snadno škálovatelný."
- After: „Tento přístup je rychlý, levný a snadno škálovatelný."

---

## 2. Structure and formatting

**Pattern:** Bullet lists where continuous prose would work fine.

Example:
- Before (in plain prose, not a technical how-to):
  „Výhody jsou:
  - rychlost
  - cena
  - jednoduchost"
- After: „Hlavní výhody jsou rychlost, cena a jednoduchost."

**Pattern:** Mid-sentence bold text to emphasize a term, even when the sentence isn't a list.

Example:
- Before: „Je **klíčové** si uvědomit, že **kontext** hraje zásadní roli."
- After: „Je důležité si uvědomit, že kontext hraje zásadní roli."

**Pattern:** Artificial headers like „Závěr" / „Shrnutí" at the end of short text or an email.

**Pattern:** Mechanical three-part structure (intro – body – summary) even for a simple answer.

---

## 3. Vocabulary and phrasing

Common stock words/phrases to avoid or replace:

| AI phrase | More natural alternative |
|---|---|
| nicméně | ale, avšak |
| nadto, navíc | a taky, k tomu |
| v neposlední řadě | a taky, a nakonec |
| je důležité poznamenat, že... | (often can be cut entirely) |
| nejde jen o X, ale také o Y | (rephrase directly, without the template) |
| klíčový, zásadní (overused) | důležitý, hlavní — or just drop it |
| ponořme se do toho / hluboký ponor | pojďme na to, podívejme se blíž |
| pokud chcete, mohu... / dejte mi vědět, kdybyste... | (often unnecessary at the end of a text) |

**Pattern:** „nejde jen o X, ale také o Y" construction.

Example:
- Before: „Nejde jen o úsporu času, ale také o zlepšení kvality práce."
- After: „Ušetří to čas a zároveň zlepší kvalitu práce."

---

## 4. Syntax and rhythm

**Pattern:** Consecutive sentences of similar length — missing natural irregularity.

**Pattern:** Tri-colon (three items listed in a row) — very common in AI text.

Example:
- Before: „Řešení je rychlé, efektivní a spolehlivé."
- After: „Řešení je rychlé a spolehlivé." (or a different phrasing without the triple)

**General fix:** Alternate short and longer sentences, occasionally use an incomplete or more casual sentence, allow for a digression.

---

## 5. Tone

**Pattern:** Overly balanced tone with no clear opinion — the text weighs "on one hand, on the other," even where taking a stance would be fine.

**Pattern:** High politeness/caution even in an informal context.

**Pattern:** Missing personal asides, small emotion, irony, or sarcasm.

**Pattern:** Ending with an offer of further help, even when nobody asked for it.

---

## Final checklist

- [ ] Removed/replaced mid-sentence dashes
- [ ] Reduced unnecessary bullet lists
- [ ] Removed mid-sentence bold emphasis
- [ ] Replaced or cut stock phrases (nicméně, v neposlední řadě, je důležité poznamenat...)
- [ ] Broken up tri-colon constructions
- [ ] Sentences vary in length and rhythm
- [ ] Text includes at least a hint of personal tone or opinion (where it fits the purpose)
- [ ] Removed closings like „dejte mi vědět, kdybyste měli zájem"
- [ ] Facts, numbers, and meaning of the text remain unchanged
